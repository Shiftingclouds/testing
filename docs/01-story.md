# Wrenfold: A School for Late Magic — the story

*The whole plan in plain prose, spoilers throughout. Start here. The world's details are in `02-world.md`, the people in `03-cast.md`, the opening film in `04-intro-storyboard.md`, and what ChatGPT needs to draw in `05-art-handoff.md`.*

## The book in one breath

You're twenty-five. You work a job you don't love in a rainy railway town called Wrexley, you live alone in a narrow house under the viaduct, and for most of this year small wrong things have been happening around you: bulbs blowing when you're angry, the kettle boiling before you've switched it on, candles leaning towards you like flowers to a window. At midnight on the last night of summer, a letter slides under your door with nobody on the other side of it. It says that your magic has begun, late, the way it does for one witch or wizard in four, and that a place has been kept for you at **Wrenfold**, the only school in the world for the late-kindled. Then a doorway of light draws itself in your kitchen wall.

From September to Midsummer you learn to hold a wand, brew, fly, ward, read the stars and keep your own fire from burning you up, in an old castle across a black lake where ten thousand paper lanterns drift under the roof of the Lantern Hall. You're sorted by a lantern into one of four houses, you choose a familiar, you make friends and one of them, maybe, becomes more. And through all of it runs the dark: the **Grey Choir**, hooded singers who can hum a person's magic out of them like a candle, and who hunt the late-kindled above everyone else, because a late flame in its first year can be taken whole.

Even the Headmistress can't keep them out. Even the Order of the Lamp can't. By Midsummer, you are the reason they're coming.

## Why a school for grown-ups

Most witches and wizards kindle as children, somewhere between six and twelve; their magic is small and steady and their families teach them at home. About one in four kindles late, between twenty-one and thirty-five, all at once, and nobody knows why.

A late flame is *bright and unset*. For about a year after it catches (**the Burning Year**) it surges, flares, and leaks: it can set a room alight, freeze a lake, or simply burn its owner out from the inside, leaving them grey and empty. Taught, it settles into something stronger than most early magic ever becomes.

So four hundred years ago **Hester Wren**, who kindled at thirty-one in a fishing village and nearly drowned a harbour, founded Wrenfold: one year, for anyone whose fire comes late. Students arrive at every age from twenty-one to thirty-four, from every kind of life: nurses, bakers, a firefighter, a law student, a mechanic, a disgraced heir, you. Nobody there was a child at school with anybody else. Everyone left something behind to come.

A second year exists for the few who want it (Idris is in his). Most leave in June with their flame settled, a wand, a licence to practise, and a life to decide.

## Your gift: the Kindler

In the Burning Year every student's flame shows off a little: some brew as if they'd always known how, some can't stop things floating. Yours does something nobody's has done in forty years.

**You can see flames.** Other people's magic shows to you as a light in the chest: a candle, a torch, a hearth, a spark. You can feel when one gutters. By October you learn you can **touch** them: steady one that's flaring, and (the thing that frightens the staff) **relight** one that has gone out. The old word is *Kindler*. Hester Wren was one. The last before you was a student called **Aldric Morrow**, forty years ago.

**Limits, so the gift never solves things by itself:**

- Seeing a flame tells you how strong it is and whether it's frightened or steady. It never tells you what anyone's thinking, whether they're lying, or how they feel about you.
- Relighting costs you. Every flame you relight takes a little of yours, and a whole relighting leaves you cold and shaking for days. You can't relight everyone.
- Hooded Choir-singers show as nothing at all: holes in the dark, as if they've hummed themselves out too.

Who you tell is yours to decide. The Headmistress asks you to tell no one.

## The dark: the Grey Choir

Forty years ago, **Aldric Morrow** was the brightest student Wrenfold had seen in a century and the first Kindler since its founder. During a Warding exercise, a classmate's panic spell went wrong and **snuffed him**: put out his flame entirely. The school hushed it up: the classmate was from an important family, the Deputy of the day wanted no scandal, and a hollowed Kindler was an embarrassment. They sent Morrow home grey and empty and told him he'd recover.

He didn't. But he was still a Kindler somewhere under the ash, and he found that while he couldn't light his own flame, he could *take* someone else's and burn it for a while. A stolen flame lasts months. Then he needs another.

He has spent forty years learning to take flames without touching anyone: with a **note**. A hummed, wordless chord that makes flames gutter and lean towards it. He taught it to the lost and the bitter and the magicless-born who wanted what others had, and they became **the Grey Choir**. Choir members wear grey, hum rather than speak, and leave the people they take **hollowed**: alive, walking, polite, grey-skinned and blank, remembering less every week. The hollowed are kept at **St Ide's**, a hospital in the city nobody visits.

Late flames in their Burning Year can be taken *whole*, in one song. That's why the Choir circles Wrenfold every year, and why the school has wards, the Lanternwarden's lanterns, and a Headmistress who doesn't sleep much.

**What Morrow wants this year:** the **Heartfire**, Hester Wren's own flame, which she left burning under the school when she died. It lights every lantern in the Lantern Hall, holds the wards, and has kept Wrenfold safe for four hundred years. A Kindler could take it out of the rock and put it in a person. Morrow can't reach it himself: he needs a living Kindler to carry it. **He needs you.**

**The Order of the Lamp** (the Lamplighters) exists to fight the Choir. They're brave, stretched thin, and not above using students as bait.

## The insider

Someone inside Wrenfold is helping the Choir: opening the wards a crack on certain nights, letting the humming in. It's **Absalom Tully, the Lanternwarden**, the gentle old man who tends the ten thousand lanterns and knows every student by name. His daughter **Maisie** was hollowed six years ago, at twenty-four, in her Burning Year. Morrow has promised to relight her once he has the Heartfire and a Kindler to carry it. Tully believes him.

The mystery points elsewhere first: at **Professor Magnus Grey**, the scarred and silent Warding master who was once a Lamplighter spy inside the Choir, and who has every reason to look guilty. He is innocent. He dies in February protecting students from the Choir, in the chapter called *The Quiet*, whatever you thought of him.

## The timeline

One school year, September to June, in 24 chapters. Placeless but British: rain, trains, chip shops, the Lantern Hall.

### Part One: Kindling (September)

**1. The Letter.** *(Thursday 10 September, night.)* The film opens it (see the storyboard): night over Wrexley, the viaduct, your lit window, the letter, the invitation, the doorway of light. Then the prose: who you are (name, witch or wizard, the life you've been living, the job), Nana Pearl across town, your friend Dev from work. The doorway will hold until dawn. You can pack one thing that matters, ring Nana, leave a note, or step straight through. You step through into Lamplight Row.

**2. Lamplight Row.** *(10–11 September.)* A hidden street of all-night shops in the city of Kingsmere. The Wren's Nest welcome office and its fussy clerk Mr Fitch. Other new students: **Toby Quill** (a baker's apprentice who's been setting croissants on fire), **Rowan Ashby** (a firefighter who walked out of a burning house unburned), **Imogen Sallow** (a law student who read the whole handbook on the train), **Saoirse Maddock** (a motorbike mechanic who kindled by fixing an engine without touching it). A wand is chosen at Pellow & Daughters, where the wand chooses the hand. Robes at Hask & Needle. A familiar at the Menagerie, or a familiar chooses you. **The first shadow:** in a side alley, three grey-hooded figures hum, and every lamp on the Row gutters. A young Lamplighter, **Jory Penrose**, moves you all along too quickly.

**3. The Lantern Hall.** *(Friday 11 September.)* The Lantern Train at dusk from Platform Nought; the boats across Wrenfold Mere in the dark; the castle; and the Hall itself: ten thousand paper lanterns drifting under a roof you can't see, humming faintly, candle-warm. **The Nesting:** each new student lights a lantern and lets it go, and it flies to one of four roosts. You can lean it. **Casimir Drummond**, the late-kindled son of an old family, is nested with an audible sneer. **Headmistress Imelda Kestrel's** speech: welcome, wonder, and one warning: *keep your flame close*.

**4. First Lessons.** *(Mid-September.)* Wordcraft with tiny, delighted Professor Moth. Brewing with Professor Kovač, who says nothing twice. Turning with Professor Bassani, who says everything twice, theatrically. Flight on old school brooms with Coach Okoro. Warding with Professor Grey, who says almost nothing and whose hands are scarred to the wrist. Your flame surges in front of everyone. **Noor Haddad**, a nurse, sits with you after. The common room; your roommate; whose side you take when Cas humiliates Toby.

**5. The Burning Year.** *(Late September.)* Night in the corridors. You see flames for the first time: the whole castle full of lights, and one guttering. You relight a snuffed candle with your bare hand, and **Idris Penhallow**, a second-year Kindling scholar, sees you do it. The Headmistress's study, the Weathervane Room: *you're a Kindler; tell no one*. A humming, deep in the walls, that nobody else can hear.

**6. The Hollowed Girl.** *(Early October.)* **Delphine Arceneaux**, a Heronmere second-year, is found in the Glasshouses at dawn, grey and polite and empty. The school shakes. Detention (for being out after curfew) with the Lanternwarden, **Absalom Tully**, who shows you how the lanterns are fed and tells you about his daughter, in St Ide's. Her lantern: snuffed, with a grey wick.

**7. An Evening Already Promised.** *(Saturday 10 October.)* The first Glimmerball match of the year, Rowan's first game; or Saoirse's forbidden workshop party in the Rookhallow Undercroft; or Noor's night shift in the Infirmary, which she asked you to sit through with her. What you promised, and to whom, matters. Toby asks **Priya** to the Emberfall lanterns.

### Part Two: Emberfall (October to December)

**8. Emberfall.** *(Saturday 31 October.)* The night the veil thins. Masks, apple-fire, lanterns floated on the Mere to the dead. Ghosts walk the cloisters for one night; one of them, **Lettice Crane**, hollowed forty years ago, walks straight up to you: *he's coming back for what he lost; you have it*. Imogen's brother is at St Ide's; she tells you, or doesn't.

**9. Under Wrenfold.** *(Early November.)* A complete adventure: the Old Cloisters beneath the school are sealed, and something in them is calling students by name; or a painted map in the Owlcombe Stacks is eating the ink out of the library. Each is its own mystery with its own monster, and neither has anything to do with the Choir, except that both were woken by something moving the old wards.

**10. The Lamplighters.** *(Mid-November.)* A second hollowing, a Rookhallow first-year, **Bram Hollis**, Cas's old crony. The Order arrives: **Commander Sabine Arkwright** and her officers take over the Warding Hall and interview everyone. Suspicion falls on Professor Grey (he was Choir once, as a spy), and on Cas (Bram's last argument was with him). Arkwright learns what you are, or doesn't, depending on who you've told.

**11. Thimble Cross.** *(Saturday 5 December.)* The first village weekend: snow, the sweetshop, the Crooked Lantern, a wand-polishing stall, a present to buy. The Choir comes into the high street in daylight. **Odile Pellow**, the wandwright, who's opened a winter stall, stands in front of the students and sings the old counter-song until they hum her out. She's hollowed, not dead. It's the first time you see what the Choir does with your own eyes, and the first time you understand the Order can't be everywhere.

**12. Longnight.** *(Friday 18 – Monday 21 December.)* The solstice: the Frost Market on the Mere, the Longnight Dance in the Lantern Hall (who you ask, who asks you), the feast at which every lantern burns blue at midnight. In the library, in Hester Wren's hand, the word *Heartfire*. Term ends. Go home for the holidays, or stay.

### Part Three: Deep Winter (January to March)

**13. Home Between.** *(Late December – early January.)* If you go home: Wrexley, Nana Pearl, Dev, your old job, your own bed, and how small and dear and strange it all is now. The Choir finds your street. If you stay: Wrenfold at Christmas, half-empty, snowed in, with whoever else stayed, and a night walk where you find the Heartfire's door, and something humming behind it.

**14. Candlewake.** *(Tuesday 2 February.)* The festival of first light: every student carries a candle across the frozen Mere at dawn and gives it to someone. It means what you want it to mean. Route peaks. Idris shows you what he's been studying: Morrow's old school file, which someone removed from the Stacks.

**15. The Glimmer Cup.** *(February.)* The inter-house tournament, in the snow. Sabotage: a lantern-hoop that snuffs the players who fly through it. Rowan falls. The saboteur used oil from the Lanternwarden's store. You notice, or you don't.

**16. What the Founders Hid.** *(Late February.)* Hester Wren's journal, Morrow's file, the Warding exercise forty years ago, the classmate who snuffed him (the old Deputy's son: Casimir's grandfather), and the Heartfire under the school. What Morrow wants and why he needs a Kindler to carry it.

**17. The Quiet.** *(Early March.)* The Choir comes inside Wrenfold at night through a ward someone opened. **Toby is hollowed.** **Professor Grey dies** holding a stair against six singers so the first-years can get out. The Headmistress is hurt. Nothing is safe, and the Order can't make it so.

**18. The Lanternwarden.** *(Mid-March.)* The clues come together: the oil, the humming in the lanterns, the grey wicks, the ledger, St Ide's. It's Tully. What you do with him: expose him to Arkwright, go to the Headmistress, or go to St Ide's yourself and look at Maisie's flame.

### Part Four: The Greening (March to June)

**19. The Greening.** *(Saturday 20 March.)* Spring, and a choice. The Order offers to take you out of school and keep you safe, as bait, somewhere the Choir will follow. Or you stay. You try to relight Toby. It's the hardest thing you've done, and it costs you.

**20. The Wren's Song.** *(April.)* The counter-song Odile Pellow sang, found in Hester Wren's journal, can't be sung by one person alone: it takes a choir of your own. You gather one, from all four houses, the people who'll stand with you because of how you've treated them all year.

**21. Brightfire.** *(Saturday 1 May.)* The spring fire festival on the hill. The last good night: the bonfire, dancing, whoever you love. Then Tully's choice, or yours: the plan to use him to draw Morrow in.

**22. Midsummer Eve.** *(Sunday 20 June.)* The Choir's assault during the Midsummer Proving. The lanterns gutter all at once. Battle in the Lantern Hall, the cloisters, the boathouse. Who stands where depends on everything you've done.

**23. The Heartfire.** *(Night of 20–21 June.)* Under the school, in the root of the rock, the founding flame, and Aldric Morrow, grey as ash, asking you to carry it for him. What you do. The endings divide here.

**24. The Last Lantern.** *(Saturday 26 June, and a year later.)* The Leaving Feast. What's lost and who's left. Where you go. An epilogue a year on, assembled from what you did.

## The endings

| | Ending | How it comes |
|---|---|---|
| A | **The Heartfire Holds** | The Wren's Song holds the Hall; you keep the Heartfire from Morrow; the Order takes him. The school stands, and you keep your flame. |
| B | **Every Lantern Lit** | You relight Morrow himself, his own flame, gone forty years. He becomes a tired old man who can do nothing but light a candle; the hollowed he took can be relit, slowly, by you and Idris. It costs you most of your fire. |
| C | **The Cold Hall** | You keep the Heartfire from Morrow by letting it go out. The Hall goes dark, the wards fall, Wrenfold must move. Morrow dies of hunger in the root. Everyone lives. |
| D | **The Last Lantern** | You give your own whole flame to relight the Heartfire after Morrow breaks it. Wrenfold stands. You go home to Wrexley without magic, remembering everything, and your friends come to visit. |
| E | **The Grey Road** | You take the Heartfire yourself. Morrow was right about how it feels. |
| F | **The Order's Oath** | Morrow escapes into the Fen with a stolen flame. You leave with the Lamplighters to hunt him. |
| G | **What We Could Save** | You couldn't stop him, only choose who comes out of the root alive: Toby, Maisie Tully, or the Headmistress. Three variants, like Calder's. |
| H | **The Road Not Taken** | You left in March with the Order, or went home in January. The last chapter is Midsummer read about in the paper, and a letter from whoever stayed. |

## The six (and Toby)

Romance is open with any of the six leads, whatever you are, and entirely optional. Nobody has a sweetheart back home. Each route has five beats and a Midsummer promise, and a *together* or *parting* epilogue.

- **Rowan Ashby** (26, Larkspire). Ex-firefighter from a big loud family. Walked out of a house fire unburned and hasn't been warm since. Brave to the point of stupid; terrified of his own flame, which comes out as heat. Route: learning to be afraid in front of someone.
- **Imogen Sallow** (24, Owlcombe). Law student who read the handbook on the train. Sharp, ambitious, wants to join the Order the day she leaves. Her brother **Kit** was hollowed three years ago. Route: from using you to needing you.
- **Saoirse Maddock** (28, Rookhallow). Motorbike mechanic, builds illegal contraptions, laughs at the wrong moments, broke every rule in her first week. Route: what she's avoiding by never sitting still.
- **Casimir Drummond** (27, Rookhallow or Owlcombe, whichever you aren't). Heir to an old magical family, disgraced by kindling late, sneering, lonely, secretly kind. His grandfather is the man who snuffed Morrow. Route: rival to friend to more, and a family debt.
- **Noor Haddad** (25, Heronmere). A&E nurse; calm, dry, good in a crisis, bad at being looked after. Kindled mid-shift by stopping a patient's bleeding with her hands. Route: letting someone carry something for once.
- **Idris Penhallow** (29, Owlcombe, second year). The Kindling scholar studying the old Kindlers; the only person who sees what you are. Quiet, exact, secretive, suspected by half the school. Route: the one who knows your secret.
- **Toby Quill** (23, Heronmere). Your best friend. Not a romance. Baker's apprentice, hapless, brave when it counts, in love with Priya. Hollowed in March. Whether and how he comes back is the heart of the last act.

## What carries across

- Four stats: **Nerve**, **Wit**, **Heart**, **Flame** (raw power), 0–100, plus **Kindling** (how far your gift is trained) and **Chill** (what relighting has cost you, 0–3).
- **House points** for all four houses, and the House Lantern at the Leaving Feast.
- Hearts with the six leads (0–6) and friendship with twenty others (0–3).
- Your **familiar**, chosen in Chapter 2, who is in every chapter and saves your life once.
- Your **wand** (wood and heartspark), which says something about you.
- **Evidence** towards the insider and Morrow's plan: 16 clues, each with two routes.
- Promises: who you promised and whether you kept it.

## How it's kept consistent

Same as Calder: `plan/` is the storyboard as checkable data (people, places, calendar, every scene with its date, place, cast and purpose), and the tools check the script against it: dates run forwards, nobody speaks before you meet them, the hollowed and the dead don't chat, everyone's where they could be. Bots play the game thousands of times to prove every option and ending is reachable.
