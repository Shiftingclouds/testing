# Wrenfold — the world

## The feel

Merry, wondrous, exciting: the first time you walk into the Lantern Hall and the lanterns turn to look at you. Warm kitchens, stone stairs that move when they're bored, snow on the Mere, sweets that hum, a familiar asleep on your textbook. And under it, a real dark edge: people are taken, a teacher dies, the grown-ups can't save everyone, and you're the reason the dark is coming.

Placeless and British: rain, trains, chip shops, radiators, tea. Magic hides in plain sight: most people never notice Lamplight Row, the Lantern Train, or the castle across Wrenfold Mere.

---

## Magic

**Your flame.** Magic is a fire inside you, literally: a light behind the breastbone that trained eyes can learn to glimpse and that you, a Kindler, see plainly. Early flames (kindled in childhood) are small and set. Late flames catch all at once, in adulthood, and burn bright and **unset** for about a year: *the Burning Year*.

**Signs of kindling:** bulbs blowing when you're upset; kettles boiling on their own; candles leaning towards you; clocks stopping at midnight; animals staring; warm hands in winter. Everyone at Wrenfold has a story about the day theirs caught.

**Wands** focus a flame the way a lens focuses light. Wood plus a **heartspark** (the core), made at Pellow & Daughters and chosen by fitting: the wand warms in the hand that suits it.

| Wood | Says | Heartspark | Says |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rowan | protective, stubborn | Wren feather | small, brave, loud |
| Blackthorn | tested by hardship | Storm-glass | quick, volatile |
| Willow | intuitive, healing | River pearl | patient, deep |
| Ash | steady, honest | Ember-coal | warm, fierce |
| Hazel | clever, curious | Moth-silk | quiet, drawn to light |
| Yew | endures, remembers | Starlight thread | dreaming, far-seeing |

**Spells** are words spoken with intent through a wand. Wordcraft teaches the words; the flame provides the push. Late flames push too hard at first.

**Familiars** are animals whose own small flames bond with yours. A familiar can find you anywhere, warns you of danger, and steadies your flame when it surges. Choices at the Menagerie: a black **cat**, a barn **owl**, a brown **hare**, a **fox** kit, a **raven**, a green **toad**, a pale-green luna **moth** the size of your hand, a **ferret**. You name it.

**Snuffing and hollowing.** A flame can be put out: by accident (rare, terrible), by exhaustion, or by the Grey Choir's hum. A snuffed person is **hollowed**: alive, polite, grey-skinned, eyes like fogged glass, forgetting a little more each week. Nobody has come back from it in living memory. A Kindler could relight them, at a cost.

## Wrenfold

A castle of pale stone and slate on a wooded island at the north end of **Wrenfold Mere**, a long black lake in hills that are on no map. Founded by **Hester Wren** four hundred years ago, for the late-kindled only. One year's course (September to June); a second year for a few scholars.

**The Lantern Hall** is the heart of it. Ten thousand paper lanterns drift under a roof so high nobody's seen it: gold, rose, green, blue. They hum very faintly at meals. They turn towards whoever's speaking at High Table. They burn blue at Longnight midnight and red at Emberfall. Every one is fed from **the Heartfire**, Hester Wren's own flame, hidden under the school; if the Heartfire gutters, so do they. When the Grey Choir is near, the lanterns lean away from them.

**The Nesting.** On their first night, each new student lights a lantern from the Lanternwarden's taper and lets it go. It rises and flies to one of four roosts in the high corners of the Hall, and that is your house. A lantern can be *leaned* by a strong wish, and the Hall lets it.

**The rules** (as read by the Deputy Head on the first night): curfew at eleven; the Old Cloisters are sealed; the Mere is not for swimming; the Whispering Wood is not for anything; familiars are not allowed in the kitchens ("this means you, Mrs Pettigrew's goat"); and *keep your flame close*.

## The four houses

Named for the four birds in Hester Wren's crest: a wren in the middle (the school) and a lark, an owl, a heron and a rook at the corners. Each house has a common room, a colour, a head, a table in the Hall, a Glimmerball team and a running tally of points; the **House Lantern** goes to the leader at the Leaving Feast.

| House | Colours | Roost & common room | Values | Head |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Larkspire** | gold and rose | A tall tower on the east side that gets the dawn first; round room of cushions and a balcony | courage, generosity, early mornings, singing badly | Prof. Aurelio Bassani (Turning) |
| **Owlcombe** | plum and silver | The Stacks under the Observatory: shelves, ladders, a ceiling of real night sky | curiosity, patience, secrets kept, stars | Prof. Vesna Kovač (Brewing) |
| **Heronmere** | sea-green and pearl | The lake cloister, half underwater, green light through the windows | stillness, care, healing, stubborn kindness | Prof. Mabli Rhys (Herbwork) |
| **Rookhallow** | black and copper | The Undercroft beneath the kitchens: forges, workbenches, warm stone | cunning, loyalty to your own, making things, mischief | Prof. Tamsin Crook (Beastlore) |

House greetings: Larkspire *"Up with the lark,"* Owlcombe *"Eyes open,"* Heronmere *"Stand still,"* Rookhallow *"Mind your own."*

## Classes

| Subject | Professor | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Wordcraft** (charms) | Dunstan Moth | The words that make things move, light, lock, mend |
| **Brewing** (potions) | Vesna Kovač | Cauldrons in the cellars; draughts, salves, the Stillwater tonic every student takes weekly |
| **Turning** (transfiguration) | Aurelio Bassani | Making one thing another; hardest subject; most dramatic teacher |
| **Warding** (defence) | Magnus Grey | Shields, counters, what to do when you hear humming |
| **Herbwork** | Mabli Rhys | The Glasshouses; mandrake-cousins called grumbles; candle-moss |
| **Starreading** | Ines Solano | The Observatory, charts, the sky's weather and what it says |
| **Beastlore** | Tamsin Crook | Familiars and the creatures of the Wood and the Mere |
| **Flight** | Coach Ferdie Okoro | Brooms, then skiffs; Glimmerball |
| **Hearth** (flame-keeping) | the Headmistress, weekly | Keeping your own fire steady; breathing; not burning out |

**Glimmerball**, the school sport: two teams of six on brooms over the Mere, one glowing ball (the Glim), three floating lantern-hoops a side. You score by throwing the Glim through a hoop; the hoop's lantern flares your house colour. The Glimmer Cup is played in the snow in February.

## Places

**Your life (Wrexley)**
- **7 Viaduct Street**: your narrow rented house under the railway viaduct: kitchen, stairs, the front door with a draught under it, a back yard with one tomato plant.
- **Nana Pearl's**: a bungalow across town that smells of lavender and toast; the only person you tell.
- **Your work**: whatever you chose (the call centre at Brindle Mutual, the A&E reception at Wrexley General, the kitchen at the Pie & Pint, the returns desk at Harker's department store, the library, the bike-courier depot).

**Lamplight Row (Kingsmere)** — a hidden street of all-night shops through a door in a bus shelter
- **The Wren's Nest**: Wrenfold's welcome office; Mr Ptolemy Fitch and ten thousand forms.
- **Pellow & Daughters, Wandwrights**: wands in drawers to the ceiling; Odile Pellow.
- **Hask & Needle**: robes, measured by a tape that gossips.
- **The Menagerie**: familiars, Ambrose Tick, a smell of hay and incense.
- **The Lamp & Ladle**: café-inn with all-night soup.
- **Platform Nought**: the Lantern Train, under Kingsmere Central, reached through a luggage office.

**Wrenfold**
- **The Lantern Hall**, the four common rooms (Larkspire Tower, the Owlcombe Stacks, the Heronmere Cloister, the Rookhallow Undercroft), the Wordcraft Gallery, the Brewing Cellars, the Glasshouses, the Observatory, the Library (the Long Stacks), the Warding Hall, the Infirmary, the Weathervane Room (the Headmistress's study), the Rookery (the familiar tower), the Boathouse and the Mere, the Glimmer Pitch, the Lanternwarden's Cottage, the Old Cloisters (sealed), the Heartfire Chamber (secret), the Whispering Wood.

**Thimble Cross** — the village at the south end of the Mere
- The high street, **Sugar & Sorcery** (the sweetshop), **the Crooked Lantern** (pub), the post office (familiar-post), the **Candlestones** on the hill.

**Elsewhere**
- **The Lamplighters' House**, Kingsmere: the Order's headquarters.
- **St Ide's**: the hospital for the hollowed, a quiet Victorian building in Kingsmere that nobody visits.
- **Saltmarrow Fen**: the drowned chapel in the marshes where the Grey Choir gathers.

## The invitation

> *Wrenfold School for Late Magic*
> *The Weathervane Room, Wrenfold, by the Mere*
>
> Dear {name},
>
> Something has happened to you this year. You have probably noticed. The kettle, the lights, the way the candles lean. You may have thought you were ill, or tired, or losing your mind. You are not. You are kindling.
>
> Your magic has begun, late, as it does for one witch or wizard in every four, and it will burn very brightly for a year and then either settle or burn you out. We would like it to settle.
>
> A place has been kept for you at Wrenfold since the day you were born, as one is kept for everyone. Term begins tomorrow evening. A doorway will open in your home at midnight and will stay open until dawn; it leads to Lamplight Row, where Mr Fitch at the Wren's Nest will see you are equipped. You need bring nothing but yourself, although most people bring a jumper.
>
> You may, of course, decline. The doorway will close at dawn, and we will not trouble you again. We would ask you to consider, before you do, what you have noticed this year, and how it has made you feel.
>
> With every good wish, and some excitement,
>
> *Imelda Kestrel*
> Headmistress
>
> *P.S. Keep your flame close.*
